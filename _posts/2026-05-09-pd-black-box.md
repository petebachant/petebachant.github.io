Agile versus waterfall, lean startup,
the product operating model.

These are all describing solutions to the same problem:

Product development is black box optimization and oftentimes the
black box part is neglected or sampled very infrequently.

Some amazing product leaders or founders can themselves serve as
a proxy for the customer response function---they are just that in tune.

Inputs:
- Money
- Dev time
- Feature ideas
- Code
- Marketing copy
- Prose

DORA describes how to properly optimize delivery neglecting the customer
response function.

That's good, but it's only half the story.

The real response we care about is money coming back into our org so we
can grow or at least sustain life.

Setting up a successful org then means measuring the right things.
They need to map to the resource inputs and

Delivery maps inputs to product features and characteristics.

The CRF maps product features and characteristics to customer response.

Customer response is often in the form of money,
but that may be a slow response, e.g.,
it may take until the end of a subscription cycle to know if a customer
churned,
and you may be able to find a faster response metric.
One obvious one would be that they stopped using the product after half
an hour.

Optimizing delivery, since it's more like a white box, is more comfortable.

Do story points correlate with revenue?

PD is black box optimization. Every sprint is one sample. KR measure response. Many of your current KR measure input. Sometimes we can only measure intermediate inputs like error rate or speed.

Waterfall is white box assumption. Implementation for nonlinearity and system decomposition as well. Pareto front.

Prototyping is surrogate model training.

CEO can serve as low order model for customer response function, but you should seek other evaluators too.

Sometimes you can be certain of the map from one delivery output to customer response. Those can be OKRs. Sometimes you need a faster approximation too.

Even customer response is an input for the funding response function.
